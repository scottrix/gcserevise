CI9: Local & Regional Government

Foundation Higher AQA 8100, OCR J270, Edexcel 1CS0

Local & Regional Government

How local government works: councils, councillors, devolved administrations, and how local decisions affect citizens' daily lives.

Key Fact: Local government: county councils, district/borough councils, and parish/community councils provide local services. Responsibilities: education (schools), social services, housing, planning permission, waste collection, local roads, libraries, and leisure centres. Funded through council tax, business rates and government grants.
Key Fact: Councillors: elected by local residents to represent their ward. They make decisions about local services, set council tax rates, and approve planning applications. Councils are led by a council leader or directly elected mayor (e.g. Mayor of London, Metro Mayors in combined authorities).
Key Fact: Devolved administrations: Scottish Parliament (129 MSPs), Welsh Senedd (60 MSs), Northern Ireland Assembly (90 MLAs) have powers over health, education, transport and environment in their nations. England does not have its own devolved parliament - English matters are decided at Westminster.
Key Fact: How local decisions affect citizens: school closures, planning permission for new housing, waste collection schedules, library opening hours, parking restrictions, and social care provision are all decided locally. Citizens can influence decisions through: voting in local elections, attending council meetings, submitting planning objections, and contacting their councillor.
Key Fact: Challenges for local government: funding cuts (austerity reduced council budgets by 20%+ since 2010), increasing demand (social care for ageing population), and limited powers (councils cannot set income tax or make criminal law). Some councils have effectively declared bankruptcy (Section 114 notices).

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Local councils = ADMINISTRATIVE (manage services). Devolved bodies = LEGISLATIVE (can pass laws). Key difference.
  • Local services: education, social care, housing, planning, waste. Funded by council tax + government grants.
  • Local government is UNDER-FUNDED and UNDER-POWERED. Needs more resources to serve citizens effectively.

📝 Exam Technique

Citizenship Studies Exam Tips — Local & Regional Government:
1. For Local & Regional Government questions, use citizenship terminology and reference democratic processes accurately
2. Consider multiple viewpoints — including those you disagree with
3. Link Local & Regional Government to current affairs and real-world examples where possible
4. When evaluating, consider both rights and responsibilities
5. Structure longer answers: identify the issue, analyse different perspectives, reach a justified view

⚠️ Common Errors

✗ Local councils are not important because all the real decisions are made in Westminster. ✓ Local councils make decisions that directly affect daily life: school quality, social care for elderly relatives, bin collection, planning permission for new buildings, library opening hours, and local road maintenance. Councils spend over £100 billion annually and employ over 2 million people. While Westminster sets national policy, local councils determine how services are delivered locally. Decisions about your street, school and neighbourhood are mostly made locally, not nationally.

✍️ Model Answer

Full-Mark Response

Evaluate whether local government should be given more powers and funding.

A grade 9 response will: describe current local government powers and funding; argue for more power (local decisions better reflect needs, devolution works for Scotland/Wales, democratic accountability is stronger locally); argue against (postcode lottery risk, national standards ensure equality, local areas may lack expertise and resources); evaluate funding crisis (austerity cuts, rising demand, Section 114 notices); conclude: local government needs significant additional funding to deliver existing responsibilities, and some additional powers (especially over transport and housing) would improve local democracy. However, strong national frameworks are needed to ensure minimum standards.

Detailed Notes

Understanding Local & Regional Government in British Context

Local & Regional Government is a core topic in GCSE Citizenship Studies that requires understanding of how the UK political and legal systems work, and how citizens can participate effectively. The specification requires you to know how British democracy functions, understand your rights and responsibilities as a citizen, and be able to take informed and responsible action on citizenship issues.

When writing about local & regional government in GCSE exams, always refer to specific UK examples: the UK Parliament at Westminster, the devolved legislatures (Scottish Parliament, Welsh Senedd, Northern Ireland Assembly), the court system (magistrates’ courts, Crown Court, High Court, Supreme Court), and real examples of active citizenship campaigns. Examiners reward answers that show understanding of how systems work in practice, not just textbook descriptions.

British Values underpin GCSE Citizenship: democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, and mutual respect and tolerance of different faiths and beliefs. Understanding local & regional government requires connecting these values to the specific systems and processes you are studying.

GCSE Example: Understanding Local & Regional Government in British Context

When discussing local & regional government, a strong GCSE answer would: define the concept precisely, explain how it operates in the UK using a specific example, evaluate its effectiveness, and consider how citizens can engage with it through active citizenship.

Local & Regional Government and UK Democratic Processes

The UK is a parliamentary democracy where citizens exercise power through elected representatives. Understanding local & regional government requires knowledge of how democratic processes work: general elections (first past the post), devolved elections (additional member system for Scotland and Wales), local elections, and referendums. The UK does not have a written constitution — instead, constitutional principles are drawn from statute law, common law, conventions and authoritative works.

The UK Parliament consists of the House of Commons (650 elected MPs), the House of Lords (appointed and hereditary members), and the monarch. A Bill becomes an Act of Parliament after passing through both Houses and receiving Royal Assent. Understanding how laws are made is essential context for local & regional government, as it determines how citizens can influence the democratic process.

Active citizenship means participating in democratic processes beyond just voting: contacting your MP, joining pressure groups, participating in consultations, organising campaigns, and using legal rights such as freedom of information requests. GCSE Citizenship requires evidence of active citizenship involvement.

GCSE Example: Local & Regional Government and UK Democratic Processes

A group of GCSE Citizenship students concerned about local & regional government could organise a campaign: research the issue, write to their MP, create a petition on the parliament.uk website, arrange a meeting with local councillors, and document the entire process as evidence of active citizenship.

Rights, Responsibilities and Local & Regional Government

UK citizens have both rights and responsibilities. Key rights include: the right to vote, freedom of speech, freedom of religion, the right to a fair trial, and the right to privacy. These rights are protected by the Human Rights Act 1998, which incorporates the European Convention on Human Rights into UK law. The Supreme Court is the highest court in the UK and can declare government actions incompatible with human rights.

Key responsibilities include: obeying the law, paying taxes, serving on a jury if called, and respecting the rights of others. Citizenship is not just about rights — it requires active participation and contribution to the community. The balance between rights and responsibilities is a key theme in GCSE Citizenship.

When evaluating local & regional government, consider different perspectives: how might different groups in society be affected differently? What are the competing rights and responsibilities? How do democratic processes ensure that different views are heard and balanced?

GCSE Example: Rights, Responsibilities and Local & Regional Government

When discussing rights and responsibilities related to local & regional government, a balanced answer would consider: what rights are at stake, who holds them, what responsibilities accompany those rights, and how the UK legal and political systems protect and enforce them.
