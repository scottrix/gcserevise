H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

Manifest Destiny, the Homestead Act 1862, the transcontinental railroad, sod-house problems and their solutions — with mechanism chains for essay questions.

📋 Key Concepts

Manifest Destiny: God intended settlement west to the Pacific — justifying expansion and ignoring Native rights. Homestead Act 1862: 160 acres free for 5 years’ work. Transcontinental railroad (1869) + land grants opened markets. Problems: sod houses, blizzards, drought, locusts (1874–75), isolation; 160 acres too little in dry lands. Solutions: barbed wire (1874), windmills, dry farming, Turkey Red wheat, Exodusters (1879).

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

'The railroads were the most important reason for settlement of the Plains.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

YES: the 1869 line brought settlers, goods and markets; land grants funded it; railhead towns boomed. NO: the Homestead Act gave the LEGAL right; barbed wire and windmills made 160 acres workable; Manifest Destiny supplied the motive. Verdict: railroads DELIVERED settlement — law and technology made it survive.

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Describe two features of the Homestead Act.

Q2: Give two problems homesteaders faced on the Plains.

Q3: Describe two features of Manifest Destiny.

Q4: Explain why 160 acres was often not enough (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: 'Barbed wire changed the West more than the railroad.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

✅ Answers

  1. Homestead Act 1862: 160 acres, 5 years, small fee (21+ or head of household).
  2. Transcontinental railroad completed 1869 (Promontory Point).
  3. Barbed wire 1874 (Glidden); windmills pumped deep water.
  4. Exodusters 1879: ex-slaves moving to Kansas.
  5. Locust plagues 1874–75; Panic of 1873 slowed settlement.

🗺️ In the Exam

Economic questions need MECHANISMS: land grant → railroad → settler → market → more settlers. The 1862/1869/1874 sequence anchors any answer.

⚡ Targeted Drills

  • Homestead Act 1862: 160 acres, 5 years, $18 fee, 21+/head of household.
  • Sod houses; buffalo-chip fuel; Turkey Red wheat; dry farming.
  • Barbed wire 1874 (Glidden); windmills (deep wells).
  • Exodusters 1879 → Kansas; railroad land sales $2–10/acre.
  • Panic of 1873 → Grange; loneliness documented by women’s diaries.

📖 Detailed Notes: Homesteader Problems and Solutions, Paired

  • Drought and low rainfall: the 100th meridian (roughly the Dakotas–Texas line) marked dry country; 160 acres (chosen for WETTER eastern land) could not support a family in the west. Solutions: dry farming (dust-mulch), deep-well windmills (self-regulating pumps), drought-resistant Turkey Red wheat brought by Mennonites from Russia.
  • Building materials: no trees on the prairie → sod houses ("soddies" — warm in winter, cool in summer, roofed leaks); rail-borne lumber once lines arrived. Fuel: buffalo and cow chips burned.
  • Fencing: wood was scarce and crops/animals wandered — barbed wire (Glidden 1874) ended open-range grazing and protected crops; fence-cutting wars followed.
  • Isolation: 20+ miles to town; women’s loneliness documented in diaries; rural churches and schools (one-room, all-ages) built community; mail-order catalogues (Montgomery Ward 1872) transformed consumption.
  • Debt and panic: machinery on credit; the Panic of 1873 and falling prices squeezed farmers → Granger movement and (later) Populism.

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: The government gave land away for nothing Correct: It was a deliberate policy to settle the Plains — and railroad companies profited hugely
  • Wrong: Homesteaders were all white-born Americans Correct: Immigrants and Exodusters (black Americans) settled too

Paper 2: Shaping the nation (one thematic study with historic environment + one British depth study including the historic environment). AQA's AO4 historic-environment questions reward site-specific knowledge, not general period knowledge.

✍️ Extended Answer Plan — "Federal policy was the most important reason the Plains were settled." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

FEDERAL: Homestead Act 1862; Pacific Railway Act (land grants); army protection; treaty-breaking that cleared Native title.

OTHER: Manifest Destiny ideology (the motive), railroad companies’ advertising and credit (the mechanism), European immigration and Exodusters (the people), barbed wire/windmills (the survival kit).

Judgement: federal policy WROTE the rules; railroads and technology played the game. Conclude: policy without railroads settles nothing; railroads without policy settle nothing legally — argue the partnership and give the last word to the mechanism (trains move people).

🔬 Case Study Focus — The Exodusters in Kansas (1879)

In 1879 thousands of black sharecroppers left Mississippi, Louisiana and Tennessee for Kansas — Benjamin ‘Pap’ Singleton’s Exodus — fleeing Reconstruction’s collapse, Jim Crow violence and the 1878 yellow-fever epidemics. ~15,000–20,000 arrived; some founded all-black towns (Nicodemus, 1877). The St Louis mayor tried to block the steamboats; Kansas’s governor, John St John, welcomed them. Exam use: the Exodusters prove the Homestead Act’s promise cut across race, BUT their harder credit and harsher town reception show the West’s limits — pair with the fact that the Plains’ 160-acre units assumed eastern rainfall.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Sequence 1862 → 1869 → 1874 as your essay spine.
  • Pair each problem with its solution (sod → lumber rails; drought → windmills).
