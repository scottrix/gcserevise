H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

The six-factor toolkit (religion, individuals, science and technology, attitudes, government, war) across every period — with interplay techniques for 16-mark thematic essays.

📋 Key Concepts: The Factor Toolkit

Every medicine essay is a FACTOR essay. Six factors cover the whole c1250–present sweep — learn an example for each in EACH period block:

FactorMedieval/RenaissanceIndustrialModern
ReligionChurch defends Galen; hospitals as careOpposition to vaccination/chloroformFewer clashes; ethics debates (IVF, transplant)
IndividualsVesalius, Harvey, ParéJenner, Snow, Pasteur, Koch, ListerFleming, Florey, Crick team, Doll & Hill

GovernmentBy-laws only1848/1875 Acts1906–11 reforms; 1948 NHS; smoking/sugar policy
WarHundred Years’ War little effectCrimea → NightingaleWWI blood/X-ray; WWII penicillin; Boer War → reforms

Interplay beats listing: top answers show factors COMBINING — e.g. germ theory (science) + epidemics (pressure) + voters (government) = the 1875 Act. The mark scheme calls this "analytical understanding of the interplay".

📝 Which Factor "Wins"? Patterns to Argue

Before 1750: religion + attitudes dominate — individuals fight uphill. 1750–1900: science + individuals lead, government follows epidemics. After 1900: government + science-industry teams dominate; individuals work INSIDE institutions. That arc is your default thesis for any "most important factor" question — adapt it to the period named and defend it with the table’s examples.

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

"Science and technology have been the most important factor in medical progress c1250–present." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

P1 (science): germ theory 1861 → everything after; DNA 1953 → genetic medicine. P2 (technology): printing spread challenge; microscope made microbes visible; HGP platforms. P3 (counter-factor): government delivered 1875/1948 regardless of technology; attitudes blocked or enabled. P4: religion/attitudes dominated the FIRST 500 years — science only became king when institutions let it. JUDGEMENT: science is the engine SINCE 1860; before that, attitudes and religion throttled even good science — so "most important overall" is false; "most important since 1860" is true.

Explain why the role of government changed after 1866 (two reasons, 4 marks).

« The 1866 cholera epidemic finally hit middle-class consumers of water-company supplies, creating political pressure. The 1867 Reform Act enfranchised urban workers, so urban health became an electoral issue. »

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Name one example of WAR accelerating medicine in each of two different periods.

Q2: Give two examples of ATTITUDES delaying medical progress.

Q3: Which factor dominated 1250–1500, and why?

Q4: Explain why "science and technology" became dominant after 1860 (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: "Religion did more harm than good to medicine c1250–present." How far do you agree? (16 + 4 marks)

✅ Answers

  1. Crimea (1853–56) → Nightingale’s sanitation revolution; WWI (1914–18) → blood depots, X-rays, Thomas splint. (Also: WWII → penicillin; Boer War → 1906–11 reforms.)
  2. Laissez-faire blocked the 1848 Act’s enforcement; religious objection delayed chloroform and Jenner; (also: trust in Galen delaying dissection).
  3. Religion — the Church controlled universities, preserved Galen and shaped hospitals; alternative voices lacked platforms (no printing).
  4. Germ theory (1861) gave science authority over tradition; new instruments (staining, Petri dishes, X-rays) produced results sceptics could see — technology made science provable.
  5. Plan: HARM — Galen preserved and enforced; dissection discouraged; vaccination/chloroform opposed. GOOD — hospitals founded and run; learning preserved; religious charity built care networks. Verdict: harm to PROGRESS, good to CARE — split the question.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Six factors with 1 example per period block (18 pairs total — build the grid as revision)
  • The arc: religion/attitudes → individuals+science → government+teams
  • Interplay sentences beat factor lists: "X created the knowledge, Y created the pressure, Z delivered it"

🗺️ In the Exam

Any 16-marker is a factor question in disguise. Recipe: P1+P2 = the two STRONGEST factors for the named change; P3 = the strongest COUNTER-factor; P4 = judgement that ranks and explains WHY that factor dominated THAT period. Quote one exact fact per factor — no factor without evidence.

🧭 Board Focus — AQA

Spec 8145. Paper 1: Understanding the modern world (one period study + one wider-world depth study). Paper 2: Shaping the nation (one thematic study with historic environment + one British depth study including the historic environment). AQA's AO4 historic-environment questions reward site-specific knowledge, not general period knowledge.

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Build the 6×3 factor grid as one revision sheet — it IS the whole thematic paper
  • In judgements, name the PERIOD limit: a factor can be "most important then, not overall"
  • Underused factors (attitudes, war) differentiate grade 8 from grade 6 — deploy them
