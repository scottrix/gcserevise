H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

Motte-and-bailey to great towers: defensive and symbolic features, building phases 1066–1135, and exam technique for the historic-environment site question.

📋 Key Concepts: Castles as Historic Environment

Prepare ONE castle across three lenses: function (motte ~5–10m, bailey = garrison/stores/court, well for sieges), phases (1066 timber → 1070s–90s stone keeps → 12th-century gatehouses/shell keeps — each phase a threat level), evidence (earthworks, keep footprints, mason marks, chapels). White Tower c1078 (Gundulf, 27m+, Caen stone) = the biggest; Pevensey 1066 = the landing fort; Durham = castle+cathedral pairing.

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

Historic-environment: "Explain how features of the castle show defensive and symbolic purposes." (AQA style)

Method: feature (motte height) → function (archery range, flood defence) → symbol (seen for miles = lordship) → phase/date link (stone keep after the 1088 rising). Site numbers are everything.

"Norman castles were built for defence, not display." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

DEFENCE: siting, storage, garrisons. DISPLAY: the White Tower’s scale beyond need; cathedral-castle pairing; the message to subjects. Verdict: early castles defend; later stone ones advertise — the function shifted as the conquest stabilised.

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Describe two defensive features of a motte-and-bailey castle.

Q2: What was the White Tower, and when was it built?

Q3: Give two features showing a castle’s administrative role.

Q4: Explain why castles changed from timber to stone (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: "Every feature of a Norman castle was military." How far do you agree? (16 + 4 marks)

✅ Answers

  1. The motte (raised mound with palisaded tower — range + flood defence); the bailey (walled yard: garrison, stables, stores, workshops).
  2. The Great Tower (White Tower), London — begun c1078 by Gundulf; largest keep in England, Caen stone shipped over.
  3. Mints and courts in the bailey; markets at the gate — tolls and administration through the lord.
  4. Fire risk (timber burns); status/permanence — stone says "here forever".
  5. Plan: motte/bailey = military; chapels, halls, market gates = lordship. Weigh by phase: 1060s defensive, 1090s symbolic.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Motte 5–10m · bailey functions · palisade → shell keep
  • White Tower c1078 (Gundulf) · Durham 1072/1093 · Pevensey 1066
  • Phases: timber → stone keeps → gatehouses (12th century)

🗺️ In the Exam

The historic-environment question is PURE site recall — no sources given. Feature → function → symbol → phase/date. Practise one castle until the numbers are automatic.

⚡ Targeted Drills

  • MotTE 5–10m; timber tower + palisade; built in DAYS with forced labour.
  • White Tower c1078 (Gundulf): 27m+ corners, Caen stone, first-floor entrance.
  • Durham 1072 castle / 1093 cathedral — peninsula site; monastic cathedral.
  • Shell keeps (Arundel, Carisbrooke) = stone palisades on old mottes.
  • Phase-reading: timber (1066–70s) → stone keep (1080s–90s) → gatehouse/tower upgrades (12th century).

📖 Detailed Notes: Reading a Castle in the Exam

  • Site choice: castles sit on high ground, river crossings, Roman roads and OLD Anglo-Saxon burhs — controlling trade and old power, not just views. Pevensey: inside a Roman Saxon Shore fort (recycled walls — Norman pragmatism). Durham: peninsula defended by river on three sides, cathedral beside castle.
  • The motte: up to 10m high, using dumped earth (sometimes a demolished Anglo-Saxon church or Roman rubble); the wooden tower on top = look-out, last refuge, lord’s hall. The bailey: stables, granary, smithy, barracks, chapel; palisade + ditch. Ringworks (bailey-only with motte-less design) appear where bedrock prevented motting.
  • Stone phases: tower keeps (Colchester c1076, White Tower c1078, Rochester 1127) — walls up to 4m thick at the base; entrance at FIRST-FLOOR level (defensive); chapel inside; well shafts through the core. Shell keeps (Arundel, Carisbrooke) convert palisades to stone circles. Gatehouses grow towers by the 12th century.
  • Symbolism: sited opposite an English minster (Norwich cathedral vs castle); bay-windowed halls in the bailey; the mound’s very shape = a permanent statement that the world had changed. Chroniclers called castles "the bones of the conquest" — today’s phrase "fossils of feudalism".

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: "stone castles immediately after Hastings" Correct: timber first — speed was the weapon
  • Wrong: "keeps were only for the lord’s family" Correct: garrisons, stores, courts, even minting in/below keeps

🧭 Board Focus — AQA

Spec 8145. Paper 1: Understanding the modern world (one period study + one wider-world depth study). Paper 2: Shaping the nation (one thematic study with historic environment + one British depth study including the historic environment). AQA's AO4 historic-environment questions reward site-specific knowledge, not general period knowledge.

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

✍️ Extended Answer Plan — "Norman castles were as much symbols as fortresses." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

FORTRESS: siting, sight-lines, garrisons, wells; motte-and-bailey’s speed was a military choice; Rochester’s cross-wall and thick base are pure defence.

SYMBOL: height over necessity (White Tower); cathedral-castle pairing; the keep visible for miles = "we are here forever"; coins and seals show castles as royal iconography.

Judgement: function and symbolism were the same decision — a castle that could not be seen deterred nobody. Conclude: Norman architecture "spoke" power; the exam answer shows the feature does both jobs at once.

🔬 Case Study Focus — Rochester Castle (1127): The Keep as Textbook

Rochester’s great tower (1127, built for the Archbishop by Gundulf’s successor William de Corbeil) is the best-documented study keep: 113ft high, walls 12ft thick at the base, cross-wall dividing it (each half independently defensible), well shaft in the forebuilding’s depth, chapel on the second floor, entrance via a forebuilding with a chapel above the gate passage. It survived the 1215 siege (mined south-east tower propped with timber props) — later rebuilt HALF round. Every feature answers the exam question "what does this feature show?" — defence (cross-wall, first-floor entry), status (chapel, Caen-quality stonework), date (round-arched Romanesque windows).

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Memorise ONE castle’s numbers — site detail is the whole mark scheme
  • Say every feature twice: "functionally X, symbolically Y"
