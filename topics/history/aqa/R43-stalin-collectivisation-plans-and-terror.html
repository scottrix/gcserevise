H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

The succession struggle, collectivisation and the 1932–33 famine, the Five-Year Plans, the Great Purge and the cult of personality.

📋 Key Concepts

Succession: Stalin’s party machine beat Trotsky (‘socialism in one country’ vs permanent revolution); Lenin’s Testament suppressed. Collectivisation (1929–33): kolkhozes, de-kulakisation, grain seized → famine 1932–33 (~5–7m dead). Five-Year Plans: Magnitogorsk, Dnieper dam, Stakhanovites; consumer goods sacrificed. Terror: Kirov murdered (1934) → Great Purge (1936–38): show trials (Zinoviev, Kamenev, Bukharin), ~750,000 executed, gulags, army officer corps beheaded. Cult of personality, Socialist Realism, Komsomol.

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

'Collectivisation failed.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

FAILED economically: output fell, famine killed millions, peasants slaughtered livestock. SUCCEEDED politically: state control of grain (exports funded industrialisation), the Party reached every village, kulaks eliminated as a class. Verdict: economic failure, political success.

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Describe two features of collectivisation.

Q2: Give two features of the Five-Year Plans.

Q3: What were the show trials?

Q4: Explain why Stalin launched the Great Purge (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: 'The Five-Year Plans succeeded.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

✅ Answers

  1. Collectivisation 1929–33; de-kulakisation; famine 1932–33 (Holodomor).
  2. Plans: Magnitogorsk, Dnieper dam, Stakhanovites 1935.
  3. Kirov 1934; show trials 1936–38; ~750,000 executed; gulags.
  4. Cult of personality; Pioneers/Komsomol; Socialist Realism.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Civil War 1918-21; Red Army ~5m; foreign intervention.
  • War Communism: requisitioning, nationalisation, trade banned.
  • Kronstadt Mar 1921; NEP 1921: tax in kind, NEPmen; famine 1921-22 (~5m).

🗺️ In the Exam

Stalin essays hold the ‘transformation vs terror’ tension: plans succeeded industrially while living standards fell — top answers keep both facts.

⚡ Targeted Drills

  • 25,000ers enforced collectivisation; kolkhoz quotas; machine tractor stations.
  • Famine 1932–33: Ukraine (Holodomor) ~3.5–5m; internal passports 1932.
  • Magnitogorsk; Dnieper dam; Stakhanov 1935 (102 tons).
  • Show trials 1936/37/38; Tukhachevsky June 1937; Yezhov → Beria.
  • Cult: Corypheus; Socialist Realism; Pavlik Morozov myth.

📖 Detailed Notes: The Machine of Transformation

  • Five-Year Plans in detail: 1st Plan (1928–32) heavy industry; Magnitogorsk built on the steppe (25,000 a month arrived); Dnieper dam (Dneprostroi); white-coal electricity; workers’ rationing and barracks; Stakhanov (1935) — 102 tons of coal in a shift, the Stakhanovite movement raising norms. 2nd/3rd Plans: chemicals, machine tools, armaments (the 3rd cut short by Barbarossa).
  • Collectivisation mechanics: 25,000ers (urban volunteers) sent to enforce; kolkhoz chairmen elected by quota; de-kulakisation — "liquidation as a class": transport in cattle trucks to Siberia (~2m deported); grain targets took seed corn → the 1932–33 famine (Ukraine hardest — the Holodomor; ~3.5–5m Ukrainian dead); internal passports (1932) chained peasants to land the serfs’ way.
  • Terror mechanics: Kirov shot (1 Dec 1934) — likely with Stalin’s knowledge; "terror railway" legislation; show trials (Zinoviev/Kamenev Aug 1936; Pyatakov Jan 1937; Bukharin/Rykov Mar 1938) — rehearsed confessions, families threatened; the Tukhachevsky case (June 1937) gutted the army (3/5 marshals, 13/15 army commanders); Yezhovshchina: ~750,000 shot (1937–38), gulag population ~2m; Yezhov himself purged (1939), replaced by Beria.
  • Culture of control: Socialist Realism (the only permitted art); history rewritten; children informed (Pavlik Morozov myth); churches dynamited or closed.

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: Collectivisation raised output Correct: It fell and caused famine — the aim was state control of grain
  • Wrong: The purges targeted only the Party Correct: Ordinary people, officers, engineers and minorities were most victims

🧭 Board Focus — AQA

Spec 8145. Paper 1: Understanding the modern world (one period study + one wider-world depth study). Paper 2: Shaping the nation (one thematic study with historic environment + one British depth study including the historic environment). AQA's AO4 historic-environment questions reward site-specific knowledge, not general period knowledge.

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

✍️ Extended Answer Plan — "Stalin’s economic policies transformed the USSR by 1939." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

TRANSFORMED: second-largest industrial power; steel/oil/electricity tripled; the Dnieper dam and new industrial cities; no unemployment (the West’s Depression contrast); tractor/tank capacity that beat Hitler.

AT WHAT COST: collectivisation’s famine (5–7m); consumption collapsed; labour camps (Belomor Canal, ~25,000 deaths); falsified statistics; consumer goods catastrophic.

Judgement: define "transformed" — the economy changed beyond recognition; the PEOPLE paid the entire bill. Conclude: transformation and terror were the same policy — the plans were built on the famine and the camps.

🔬 Case Study Focus — Magnitogorsk: Building Socialism on the Steppe

The magnet mountain: a purpose-built steel city rising from 1929 — 25,000 arrivals a month, barracks and mud, foreign engineers (Ford built the car plant), Stakhanovite heroics, totalitarian planning. By 1934 Magnitka produced more steel than all Tsarist Russia. Living conditions: queues, lice, criminal chaos — then proper blocks for the elite. John Scott’s memoir (Behind the Urals) records the heroism AND the horror. Exam use: Magnitogorsk answers "did the plans succeed?" with industrial triumph + human cost in one place — the two-sided verdict in a single city.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • 1934 Kirov → 1936–38 terror: memorise the sequence.
  • Collectivisation = politics over economics — the ready-made two-sided verdict.
