H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

Lakota bands and tribes, the buffalo economy, counting coup and beliefs, the horse — with structure vocabulary for way-of-life questions.

📋 Key Concepts

Structure: bands (extended family, ~10–30 tipis) formed tribes (Lakota, Cheyenne) only for summer hunts; chiefs led by consent; women owned the tipi and processed buffalo; old age = wisdom. Beliefs: Wakan Tanka (Great Spirit), the sacred circle, land held in common (never owned), visions and the Sun Dance. Buffalo = life: food, tipi, clothing, tools, fuel, ceremony. Warfare: small-scale and ritual — counting coup (touching an enemy) outranked killing. Horses (1600s+): transformed hunting, warfare and wealth.

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

Describe two features of Plains Indian beliefs (4).

« They believed in Wakan Tanka, the Great Spirit present in all things. The circle was sacred — tipis, camps and the Sun Dance were circular because life had no end. »

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Name two roles of women in Plains bands.

Q2: Describe two features of the Sun Dance.

Q3: What was counting coup, and why did it matter?

Q4: Describe two uses of the buffalo.

Q5: 'The horse was the most important change in Plains life.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

✅ Answers

  1. Women: owned the tipi, butchered and processed buffalo, influenced band decisions.
  2. Sun Dance: summer renewal ceremony — later banned by the US government.
  3. Counting coup: touching an enemy with a coup stick earned the highest honour.
  4. Buffalo: food, tipi covering, clothing, shields, fuel (dung), ceremony.
  5. Land: held in common and sacred — US ownership law violated the Plains way.

🗺️ In the Exam

['Homestead Act 1862: 160 acres, 5 years; railroad 1869; barbed wire 1874.', 'Band (10-30 tipis) -> tribe; chiefs by consent; women owned the tipi.', 'Counting coup outranked killing; buffalo = economy + religion; land held in common.']

⚡ Targeted Drills

  • Band 10–30 tipis; tribe = summer alliance (Seven Council Fires).
  • Women owned the tipi; could veto a chief by decamping.
  • Counting coup = touch, not kill; war societies policed hunts.
  • Wakan Tanka; the circle; visions sought at the Sun Dance.
  • Horses = wealth; bride-wealth paid in horses.

📖 Detailed Notes: Bands, Beliefs and the Horse

  • Bands: named after a leader’s quality (e.g. "Sore Back Band"); members could leave — consent ran both ways; band chiefs chosen for generosity, bravery and wisdom; a council of elders and societies (e.g. fox soldiers) kept order on the summer camp. Tribal councils (the Seven Council Fires of the Lakota) met rarely — usually for war or the Sun Dance.
  • Women’s power: the tipi AND its contents were the woman’s property; women decamped the band if they lost faith in the chief; they tanned hides, butchered, raised children, ran the home — and traded (pemmican). Marriages arranged with bride-wealth in horses.
  • War as institution: war societies (Badgers, Foxes) policed the hunt; coups counted higher than kills; prisoners often adopted (losing a son meant gaining one). War was about HONOUR, not territory — the fatal cultural mismatch with US total war.
  • The Sun Dance: annual renewal — dancing around the sacred pole, sometimes self-torture (Sun Dance Piercing) for visions; banned by Washington in the 1880s as part of assimilation.
  • The horse (Spanish origin, spread 1600s–1750): a warrior’s wealth counted in horses (stolen from other bands = double honour); hunting radius exploded; the Comanche became a horse empire.

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: Plains people were 'primitive' Correct: Their society solved survival on the Plains elegantly — mobility, consent and ecology in balance
  • Wrong: Chiefs ordered everyone about Correct: Leadership was by consent; war chiefs led only raids

✍️ Extended Answer Plan — "Plains Indian life was shaped by the buffalo more than by religion." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

BUFFALO: every material need (food, tipi, clothing, tools, fuel); the hunt structured the calendar, the band and the warfare.

RELIGION: the buffalo WAS sacred (Wakan Tanka’s gift); the Sun Dance, circles, visions and counting coup shaped law, honour and identity — immaterial but organising.

Judgement: the buffalo shaped HOW they lived; religion explained WHY — and the White assault on both (hide hunting + Sun Dance ban) proves the two were inseparable. Conclude: separating them is the settlers’ mistake; the strongest answers refuse it.

🔬 Case Study Focus — The Sun Dance and the Sacred Circle

The annual Sun Dance (summer, when bands gathered): a circular lodge around a sacred cottonwood pole; dancers faced the sun for days without food or water; some pierced their chest muscles with skewers (Sun Dance piercing) as sacrifice for visions — personal suffering for the people’s renewal. Everything about the ritual encoded the worldview: the circle, Wakan Tanka’s presence in all things, the primacy of the collective over the individual. The US government banned the Sun Dance (and -piercing) in the 1880s as part of assimilation — banning a CEREMONY was an attempt to kill a worldview. Exam use: the ban is the perfect evidence that Washington understood the culture’s strength lay in its beliefs, not just its warriors.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Learn band → tribe structure with ONE example (Lakota).
  • Buffalo + horse = your two evidence anchors for any way-of-life question.
