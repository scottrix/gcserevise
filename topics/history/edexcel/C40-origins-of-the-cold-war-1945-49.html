H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

Yalta and Potsdam, ideological distrust, the Iron Curtain, Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan, the Berlin blockade and airlift, and NATO.

📋 Key Concepts

Mistrust: the Second Front delay, Katyn, capitalism vs communism. Yalta (Feb 1945): zones, UN, free elections promised for Poland. Potsdam (Jul–Aug 1945): Truman (atomic) + Attlee; rows over reparations and borders. Soviet takeover of Eastern Europe 1945–48 (Czechoslovak coup 1948) → Iron Curtain speech (Fulton, Mar 1946). Containment: Truman Doctrine (Mar 1947, Greece/Turkey) + Marshall Plan ($13bn) → Cominform (1947)/Comecon (1949). Berlin blockade and airlift (Jun 1948–May 1949): currency reform, 278,000 flights; NATO (Apr 1949); FRG/GDR founded 1949.

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

'The Berlin blockade was the first real crisis of the Cold War.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

YES: the first direct military stand-off; NATO followed directly. NO: ideology was already at war by 1947 (Doctrine, Marshall, Czechoslovak coup). Verdict: the first MILITARY crisis of a war that began politically in 1947.

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Describe two features of Yalta.

Q2: Give two features of the Marshall Plan.

Q3: Describe two features of the Berlin airlift.

Q4: Explain why the USSR blockaded Berlin (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: 'The Truman Doctrine caused the Cold War.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

✅ Answers

  1. Yalta Feb 1945; Potsdam Jul–Aug 1945; Fulton Mar 1946.
  2. Truman Doctrine Mar 1947; Marshall Plan $13bn; Cominform 1947; Comecon 1949.
  3. Blockade Jun 1948–May 1949; 278,000 flights; NATO Apr 1949; FRG/GDR 1949.
  4. Czechoslovak coup Feb 1948.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Collectivisation 1929-33; famine 1932-33 (Holodomor).
  • Plans: Magnitogorsk, Dnieper dam, Stakhanovites 1935.
  • Kirov 1934; show trials 1936-38; ~750,000 executed; gulags.

🗺️ In the Exam

Origins essays: who was to blame? Argue actions-and-reactions (the spiral model) along the timeline Yalta → Potsdam → Fulton → Doctrine → Marshall → blockade.

⚡ Targeted Drills

  • Yalta: zones + UN + promised elections; Potsdam: Truman/Attlee + reparations rows.
  • Fulton Mar 1946 ("From Stettin in the Baltic to Trieste in the Adriatic").
  • Truman Doctrine Mar 1947 ($400m Greece/Turkey); Marshall $13bn; Czech coup Feb 1948.
  • Blockade 24 Jun 1948–12 May 1949; 1,500 flights/day peak; NATO 4 Apr 1949.
  • FRG May 1949 / GDR Oct 1949 — two Germanies.

📖 Detailed Notes: Conferences and Crises, Line by Line

  • Yalta (4–11 Feb 1945): Germany into four zones; free elections PROMISED in Poland; the Lublin (Soviet) Poles to be "broadened"; the UN agreed (USSR a permanent Security Council member). Stalin kept his word on nothing Poland-related — the Katyn truth was already known in London.
  • Potsdam (17 Jul–2 Aug 1945): Truman had the bomb (tested 16 Jul); Attlee replaced Churchill mid-conference; the Oder-Neisse line de facto; reparations taken by each occupier from their own zone (the USSR stripped its zone — and East Germany — bare); Nazis demilitarised/denazified.
  • The Iron Curtain in detail: 1945–48 coups and rigged votes: Romania (Feb 1947), Poland (Jan 1947 referendum fraud), Hungary (1947 "salami tactics" against Smallholders), Czechoslovakia (Feb 1948 — the only genuine democracy, lost last); Tito’s Yugoslavia expelled from Cominform (1948) — proof communism was not monolithic.
  • Berlin blockade mechanics: 23 June 1948 the Deutschmark introduced in the western zones; Stalin closed road/rail/canal on 24 June; 2.2m West Berliners needed 4,000 tonnes/day; the airlift peaked at 1,500 flights/day (Operation Vittles); Stalin lifted it 12 May 1949 — the Allies kept their zone and founded the FRG (May 1949); the GDR answered (October 1949); NATO (4 April 1949).

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: The Marshall Plan was charity Correct: It was economic containment — aid tied to capitalist trade, openly anti-communist
  • Wrong: Berlin was deep in the West Correct: It lay 100 miles inside the Soviet zone — hence the blockade’s leverage

🧭 Board Focus — Pearson Edexcel

Spec 1HI0. Paper 1: Thematic study with historic environment. Paper 2: Period study + British depth study. Paper 3: Modern depth study. Three papers means three distinct revision plans — Edexcel's source/interpretation marks differ per paper, so practise each paper's question stems separately.

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

✍️ Extended Answer Plan — "Stalin was more to blame than Truman for the Cold War." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

STALIN: broke the Yalta promise (Polish elections); salami tactics; rigged Czechoslovakia; the blockade as first military move; Cominform discipline.

TRUMAN: the atomic monopoly as diplomacy; doctrine of permanent confrontation; Marshall Aid as economic containment that Stalin could not accept; NATO’s alliance.

STRUCTURALISTS: two superpowers, one ideological planet — conflict was baked in; personalities only timed it.

Judgement: Stalin’s actions were the provocations; Truman’s responses were escalations in kind. Conclude: "more to blame" is the wrong question — but the FIRST unprovoked move (Czechoslovakia, the blockade) weighs on Stalin’s side.

🔬 Case Study Focus — Czechoslovakia, February 1948: The Last Democracy Falls

Czechoslovakia was the one Eastern European democracy with genuine multiparty politics and a pro-Western public. Gottwald’s communists (38% vote, 1946) took power by SALAMI TACTICS then finished it: armed workers’ militias, police purges, the non-communist ministers’ resignations trapped by their own tactics, and armed action on 25 February. Jan Masaryk’s window death (10 March) marked the end. Truman pushed the Marshall Plan through Congress in WEEKS; the West’s lesson: containment needed alliances (NATO, April 1949). Exam use: Czechoslovakia is the coup that converted US public opinion — the Cold War’s point of no return for many Americans.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • 1947 = the ideological declaration year (Doctrine + Marshall + Cominform).
  • Berlin 1948–49 = first stand-off; NATO = the consequence.
