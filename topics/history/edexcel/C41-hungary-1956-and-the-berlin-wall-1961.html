H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

De-Stalinisation, Nagy's reforms and the Soviet invasion, the West's inaction, Khrushchev's Berlin ultimatum and the Wall.

📋 Key Concepts

Hungary: Stalin died 1953; Khrushchev’s Secret Speech (Feb 1956); Nagy reformed, then announced leaving the Warsaw Pact (1 Nov) → Soviet invasion (4 Nov, ~3,000 Hungarians + ~7,000 Soviet dead); Nagy executed (1958); the West did nothing (Suez distracted; Hungary was inside the Soviet sphere). Berlin: ultimatums (1958, 1961) failed; ~3m East Germans fled west by 1961 → the Wall (13 Aug 1961) — barbed wire → concrete; escapers shot; Kennedy’s ‘Ich bin ein Berliner’ (Jun 1963). The Wall ended the refugee crisis — propaganda disaster, practical victory.

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

'The West's response to Hungary 1956 proved the Cold War respected spheres of influence.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

YES: no military aid despite Radio Free Europe’s encouragement; Suez overlap; Eastern Europe accepted as Soviet. NO: outrage, UN debates, refugee support. Verdict: spheres won — the test case for Czechoslovakia 1968 and Poland 1980.

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Describe two features of the Hungarian uprising.

Q2: Give two reasons the West did not help Hungary.

Q3: Describe two features of the Berlin Wall.

Q4: Explain why Khrushchev built the Wall (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: 'The Wall was a Soviet defeat.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

✅ Answers

  1. Secret Speech Feb 1956; Nagy PM Oct 1956; invasion 4 Nov 1956; Nagy executed Jun 1958.
  2. ~200,000 Hungarian refugees; ~3m East Germans fled by 1961.
  3. Wall 13 Aug 1961; ~140km; at least ~140 killed escaping.
  4. Kennedy Berlin speech Jun 1963.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Yalta Feb 1945; Potsdam Jul-Aug 1945; Fulton Mar 1946.
  • Truman Doctrine Mar 1947; Marshall Plan $13bn; Cominform 1947; Comecon 1949.
  • Blockade Jun 1948-May 1949 (278,000 flights); NATO Apr 1949; FRG/GDR 1949.

🗺️ In the Exam

Both crises teach the same lesson: each superpower kept to its sphere. Contrast the Wall (practical fix) with the 1958 ultimatum (failed bluff): won the point, lost the propaganda.

⚡ Targeted Drills

  • Poznañ June 1956 → Budapest 23 Oct → neutrality 1 Nov → invasion 4 Nov.
  • Mindszenty in the US embassy 15 years; Nagy hanged Jun 1958.
  • Ultimatum Nov 1958 failed; ~3m fled; Wall 13 Aug 1961.
  • Peter Fechter Aug 1962; Checkpoint Charlie tank stand-off Oct 1961.
  • Kennedy: 'Ich bin ein Berliner' Jun 1963.

📖 Detailed Notes: Two Crises in Detail

  • Hungary timeline: Riots in Poland (Poznañ, June) emboldened Budapest; 23 Oct students demonstrated (statue of Stalin pulled down — only his boots remained); Nagy returned as PM; 28 Oct Soviet troops withdrew; 1 Nov Nagy announced NEUTRALITY and Warsaw Pact withdrawal; 4 Nov 60,000 Soviet troops + tanks (Operation Whirlwind, Konev) crushed the resistance (Coravin Cinema, Kilian Barracks); Cardinal Mindszenty sheltered in the US embassy (15 years); ~200,000 refugees; Nagy kidnapped from Yugoslav embassy, tried secretly, hanged June 1958.
  • The West’s dilemma: Eisenhower refused "rollback" that meant world war; Radio Free Europe had ENCOURAGED the rising — moral responsibility without capability; Suez (31 Oct–6 Nov) split the alliance precisely when Hungary burned.
  • Berlin in detail: Khrushchev’s Nov 1958 ultimatum (six months to a free city) — expired, bluffed, repeated; the "brain drain": doctors, engineers, teachers (the GDR trained them); 13 Aug 1961 bars barbed wire overnight; two days later concrete; Ulbricht’s phrase "no one intends to build a wall" (June 1961); Peter Fechter bled to death at the wall (17 Aug 1962) — the image of the era; Checkpoint Charlie tank stand-off (Oct 1961 — 16 hours, tanks 100m apart).

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: The West sent troops to Hungary Correct: Only radio encouragement and refugee aid — deliberately no intervention
  • Wrong: The Wall stopped Western spies Correct: Its purpose was stopping East German EMIGRATION

🧭 Board Focus — Pearson Edexcel

Spec 1HI0. Paper 1: Thematic study with historic environment. Paper 2: Period study + British depth study. Paper 3: Modern depth study. Three papers means three distinct revision plans — Edexcel's source/interpretation marks differ per paper, so practise each paper's question stems separately.

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

✍️ Extended Answer Plan — "Khrushchev handled both the Hungarian uprising and the Berlin Wall badly." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

HUNGARY — badly: the invasion handed the West a propaganda gift for a generation; Nagy’s execution martyred a communist; world communism fractured (Western communist parties left).

BERLIN — well (short term): the ultimatum failed but the Wall solved the emigration crisis overnight WITHOUT war; the GDR stabilised for 28 years.

Judgement: judge by objectives: Hungary = objective (keep the empire) achieved at huge reputational cost; Berlin = objective (stop the drain) achieved at small cost. Conclude: badly on paper, effectively in practice — the dilemma between propaganda and power is the essay.

🔬 Case Study Focus — Peter Fechter and the Wall's Arithmetic

17 August 1962: 18-year-old Peter Fechter, shot in the pelvis trying to cross near Checkpoint Charlie, bled to death over an hour in the death strip while guards on both sides refused to help (East: orders; West: shots kept rescuers back). The photo went round the world; "Ihr Mörder!" (your murderers) chants met the Western garrison. At least 140 killed at the Wall over 28 years. Exam use: Fechter humanises the Wall’s arithmetic — 3m emigrants before 1961, ~140 deaths after — the Wall SOLVED the GDR’s demographic crisis by killing individuals; that trade-off is the essay’s heart.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Hungary = the sphere-of-influence test case.
  • The Wall = crisis ENDER, not starter — say why that mattered.
