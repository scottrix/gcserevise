H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

Penicillin and the antibiotics revolution, blood transfusion, the NHS 1948, DNA and the Human Genome Project, IVF, keyhole surgery and mRNA vaccines — with the government factor after 1900.

📋 Key Concepts: Modern Medicine (1900–present)

After 1900 medicine accelerated: germ theory was applied (vaccines, antibiotics, "magic bullets"), the NHS (1948) universalised access, and DNA (structure 1953, Human Genome Project 2000–03) opened predictive medicine. Two world wars forced speed: blood transfusion, X-rays in the field, plastic surgery, penicillin mass-production.

📝 The British Government After 1900

Liberal reforms 1906–11: school meals (1906), medical inspections (1907), National Insurance (1911) — the state accepted that poverty (including illness) needed intervention, partly from Boer War recruitment failures. 1948 NHS: free at the point of use, funded by taxation; the piecemeal panel system replaced. Debate ever since: cost, "health tourism", privatisation — but the principle has held for 75+ years.

Examiner’s pointer: the government factor returns in force after 1900 — wars (Boer, WWI, WWII) repeatedly exposed ill health and accelerated state action. Use that chain: war → evidence → reform.

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

"War has been the most important factor in medical progress since 1900." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

YES: WWI — blood depots (first at Cambrai, 1917), X-rays, Thomas splint (fracture deaths fell), plastic surgery (Gillies); WWII — penicillin mass-produced (US government funding), blood transfusion services (National Blood Service founded 1946 from wartime service). NO: peacetime science — DNA (1953), HGP, IVF, mRNA vaccines (2020) came from laboratory collaboration, not battlefields; the NHS came from politics, not war. JUDGEMENT: war accelerated APPLYING known science; peace-time science produced the fundamental breakthroughs — agree "partly".

Describe two features of the NHS when it opened in 1948 (4).

« It provided care free at the point of use for everyone, funded from taxation. It was built on the 1942 Beveridge Report’s plan to attack the five giants, uniting hospitals, GPs and public health under one system. »

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Name the scientist who discovered penicillin in 1928 and the pair who mass-produced it.

Q2: Describe two features of the NHS established in 1948.

Q3: Give two ways WWI improved surgery and treatment.

Q4: What did the Human Genome Project achieve, and when?

Q5: Explain why the government created the NHS in 1948 (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q6: "Penicillin changed medicine more than the NHS." How far do you agree? (16 + 4 marks)

✅ Answers

  1. Alexander Fleming (1928, mould killing staphylococci); Howard Florey and Ernst Chain (with Norman Heatley’s engineering) mass-produced it 1939–45.
  2. Free at the point of use, tax-funded; brought hospitals, GPs and public health into one national service (Beveridge Report 1942 backdrop).
  3. Blood transfusion (Ramsgate depot 1917, cross-matching after Landsteiner 1901) and the Thomas splint (fracture mortality from ~80% to ~20%); also X-ray units at casualty clearing stations, Gillies’ plastic surgery at Sidcup.
  4. Sequenced all human genes (2000–03) — enabling genetic testing, targeted drugs and the understanding of inherited disease.
  5. Beveridge identified disease as one of the "five giants"; wartime experience of a coordinated Emergency Hospital Service proved national provision worked and was popular.
  6. Plan: penicillin = global lives saved (infections that killed for millennia); NHS = access for everyone (delivery). Argue: discovery vs delivery; the strongest answers note both needed the other — antibiotics without the NHS reach fewer patients; the NHS without antibiotics treats little. Commit to a weighted verdict.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Landsteiner blood groups 1901 · Fleming penicillin 1928 · Florey/Chain 1939–45
  • Beveridge 1942 → NHS 5 July 1948 (Bevan)
  • DNA double helix 1953 (Watson, Crick, Franklin, Wilkins) · HGP 1990–2003
  • IVF: Louise Brown 1978 (Steptoe & Edwards)
  • Liberal reforms: 1906 meals, 1907 inspections, 1911 National Insurance
  • mRNA COVID vaccines 2020 — fastest vaccine development ever

🗺️ In the Exam

Modern questions reward judgement about ACCELERATION: change is fast, multi-factor and government-driven. For "greatest impact" questions, weigh scale (NHS), science (penicillin/DNA) and prevention (vaccination, smoking policy 1950s–2000s). Keep one eye on CONTINUITY: inequalities persisted (2020 COVID mortality gaps echo 1842 Chadwick) — top-band answers notice that.

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: "Fleming mass-produced penicillin" Correct: Fleming DISCOVERED it (1928); Florey, Chain and Heatley mass-produced it (1939–45, with US funding)
  • Wrong: "the NHS began in 1942" Correct: Beveridge REPORT 1942; NHS opened 5 July 1948
  • Wrong: "Watson and Crick discovered DNA alone" Correct: built on Rosalind Franklin’s X-ray crystallography (Photo 51)

🧭 Board Focus — Pearson Edexcel

Spec 1HI0. Paper 1: Thematic study with historic environment. Paper 2: Period study + British depth study. Paper 3: Modern depth study. Three papers means three distinct revision plans — Edexcel's source/interpretation marks differ per paper, so practise each paper's question stems separately.

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Chain the 20th century: war → science → state: Boer War → Liberal reforms; WWI → transfusion/surgery; WWII → penicillin + NHS momentum
  • Use scale numbers sparingly but well: NHS covers ~67 million people today
  • For factor essays, "science and technology" (microscope → DNA sequencer) is the backbone factor of this period
