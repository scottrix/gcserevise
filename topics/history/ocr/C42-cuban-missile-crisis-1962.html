H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

Castro's revolution, the Bay of Pigs, Soviet missiles, the thirteen days, the naval quarantine, the secret Jupiter deal, and the hotline and Test Ban Treaty.

📋 Key Concepts

Cuba: Castro 1959 → nationalisations → embargo → Soviet alliance. Bay of Pigs (Apr 1961): CIA-backed invasion fiasco → full Soviet tilt. Khrushchev stationed R-12 missiles in Cuba (deterrence + bargaining + US Jupiters in Turkey). U-2 photos 14 Oct 1962 → ExComm → naval quarantine (22 Oct); Black Saturday (27 Oct): U-2 shot down, submarine depth charges; secret deal: missiles out ↔ no invasion + Jupiters quietly removed from Turkey. Consequences: hotline (1963), Partial Test Ban Treaty (Aug 1963); Khrushchev ousted (1964); both sides learned brinkmanship’s danger.

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

'Kennedy won the Cuban Missile Crisis.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

PUBLIC win: Soviet missiles left; quarantine held. PRIVATE Khrushchev win: no-invasion pledge + Jupiter removal (secret for decades). Verdict: settled, not won — that lesson produced the hotline and détente.

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Describe two features of the Bay of Pigs.

Q2: Give two reasons Khrushchev placed missiles in Cuba.

Q3: Describe two features of the naval quarantine.

Q4: Explain why the crisis ended (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: 'The Cuban Missile Crisis made the Cold War safer.' How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

✅ Answers

  1. Castro 1959; Bay of Pigs Apr 1961; U-2 photos 14 Oct 1962; quarantine 22 Oct 1962.
  2. Black Saturday 27 Oct 1962.
  3. Secret deal: Jupiters removed within months; no-invasion pledge.
  4. Hotline 1963; Test Ban Aug 1963; Khrushchev ousted Oct 1964.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Secret Speech Feb 1956; invasion 4 Nov 1956; Nagy executed Jun 1958.
  • ~200,000 Hungarian refugees; ~3m East Germans fled by 1961.
  • Wall 13 Aug 1961; ~140km; ~140+ killed escaping; Kennedy speech Jun 1963.

🗺️ In the Exam

ExComm structure: options (air strike vs blockade), the quarantine’s legal cleverness, the secret trade. 'Settled, not won' is the verdict line.

⚡ Targeted Drills

  • U-2 photos 14 Oct; Kennedy briefed 16 Oct; TV address 22 Oct.
  • Quarantine line 24 Oct; ships stop 25 Oct.
  • Two letters 26/27 Oct; Trollope ploy; RFK-Dobrynin deal.
  • Black Saturday: Anderson’s U-2; B-59 depth charges.
  • Hotline 30 Aug 1963; Test Ban Aug 1963; Khrushchev out Oct 1964.

📖 Detailed Notes: Thirteen Days, Hour by Hour

  • Background chain: Castro’s victory (Jan 1959) → US embargo (1960) → Bay of Pigs (17 Apr 1961) → Operation Anadyr (Soviet deployment, Sep 1962 — R-12s with 1-megaton warheads, 42,000 troops). 14 Oct: Major Heyser’s U-2 photographs San Cristóbal; 16 Oct Kennedy briefed ("we have some photographs").
  • ExComm options: (1) do nothing; (2) diplomatic pressure; (3) secret approach to Castro; (4) invasion; (5) surgical air strike; (6) blockade — chosen as the middle option (legally "quarantine" — blockade is an act of war); OAS endorsement obtained.
  • The 13 days: 22 Oct TV address; 24 Oct Soviet ships stop/turn at the line; 25 Oct Stevenson at the UN ("until hell freezes over"); 26 Oct Khrushchev’s first letter (withdrawal for a pledge); 27 Oct the second letter (Turkish missiles) + Black Saturday — U-2 downed (Major Anderson killed), B-59 submarine depth-charged (considered a nuclear torpedo), U-2 strayed into Siberian airspace; Kennedy answered the FIRST letter and ignored the second; the Trollope ploy; RFK-Dobrynin night meeting sealed the Jupiter trade.
  • Aftermath: missiles crated and shipped; UN inspection refused; the hotline (30 Aug 1963); Test Ban Treaty (Moscow, Aug 1963); Khrushchev celebrated quietly then fell (Oct 1964 — blamed for Cuba among other things); Castro felt betrayed ("the missiles were ours to decide").

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: Kennedy's advisers all wanted war Correct: ExComm split — the blockade was the middle option over the Joint Chiefs’ air strike
  • Wrong: Khrushchev backed down for nothing Correct: He got the Jupiter removal and a no-invasion pledge — secret but real

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

✍️ Extended Answer Plan — "Mutually Assured Destruction made the world safer." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

SAFER: after Cuba, both sides built communication (hotline), agreements (test ban, non-proliferation) and restraint (quarantine not strike); no direct superpower war 1945–91.

MORE DANGEROUS: the arms race multiplied warheads (US 1967: 31,000); near-misses (1962; Able Archer 1983; Petrov 1983); proxies fought the hot wars (Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan).

Judgement: MAD prevented superpower war while privatising violence to the Third World and gambling the species on command systems. Conclude: safer for Moscow and Washington, not necessarily for the planet — the exam’s strongest two-sided verdict.

🔬 Case Study Focus — Black Saturday, 27 October 1962

The most dangerous day in human history, three times over: (1) Major Rudolf Anderson’s U-2 shot down over Cuba (killed — against Khrushchev’s standing order); (2) the B-59 Soviet submarine, depth-charged by US destroyers and out of contact, needed the two arming keys for a nuclear torpedo — Vasili Arkhipov (flotilla commander) refused consent; (3) a U-2 strayed over Siberia, fighters scrambled with nuclear air-to-air missiles. Kennedy: "there is always some sonofabitch who doesn’t get the word". The same day, Kennedy answered Khrushchev’s FIRST letter, ignored the second, and sent RFK to Dobrynin with the Jupiter trade. Exam use: Black Saturday converts "the crisis was dangerous" into evidence — three separate near-launches in one day.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Black Saturday detail shows how close it came — one well-told anecdote lifts a 16-marker.
  • Consequences ARE the evaluation: hotline, test ban, détente logic.
