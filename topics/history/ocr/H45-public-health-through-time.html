H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

From medieval by-laws and quarantine to Chadwick, the Public Health Acts of 1848 and 1875, the NHS and lifestyle policy — the five-step government story and the battle against laissez-faire.

📋 Key Concepts: Public Health Through Time

Public health = organised community action on health (water, sewers, housing, food, vaccines). The long arc: medieval towns did quarantine and street-cleaning by-laws (miasma logic) but no systematic provision; 1348/1665 plagues produced temporary action; industrial cities created the crisis; 1842 → 1848 → 1866 → 1875 → 1948 is the government story.

📝 The Government Story in Five Steps

1) 1842: Chadwick’s Report on the Sanitary Condition of the Labouring Population — filth causes disease, disease causes poverty (poor relief costs!). 2) 1848: first Public Health Act — councils MAY act (only ~180 did). 3) 1854: Snow’s Broad Street + the Board of Health abolished the next year (opinion still miasma/laissez-faire). 4) 1866: last cholera epidemic hits the water companies’ customers — the political dam breaks. 5) 1875: Public Health Act — clean water, sewers, inspectors, housing standards COMPULSORY everywhere. From 1948, health care itself joins public health; from the 1950s, prevention shifts to lifestyle (smoking: Doll & Hill’s 1950/54 studies → health warnings → 2007 indoor ban).

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

"The 1875 Public Health Act mattered more than any individual." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

YES: compulsion reached millions; Snow/Pasteur proved points but laws delivered pipes. NO: without Chadwick’s report (1842) there is no 1848 Act; without Snow/Pasteur/Koch, no evidence base; without cholera epidemics, no pressure. JUDGEMENT: the Act was the DELIVERY mechanism; individuals + epidemics were the engine. Best answers weigh mechanism vs engine.

Compare responses to the Black Death (1348) and the Great Plague (1665). (8 marks)

Similarities: prayer/penance, miasma-avoidance (herbs, smoke), quarantine attempts. Differences: 1665 had state organisation (searchers of the dead, weekly bills of mortality, shuttering infected houses, plague doctors), plus European quarantine learning. Both periods still blamed God/planets and had no effective cure.

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: What did Chadwick argue in his 1842 report, and what did it lead to?

Q2: Give two differences between the 1848 and 1875 Public Health Acts.

Q3: Describe two features of government action during the 1665 plague.

Q4: What did Doll and Hill establish in the 1950s?

Q5: Explain why the 1875 Act happened when it did (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q6: "Public health improved when government stopped believing in laissez-faire." How far do you agree? (16 + 4 marks)

✅ Answers

  1. That filth caused disease and disease caused poverty (costly poor relief); it led to the 1848 Public Health Act (optional boards of health).
  2. 1848: optional (councils could adopt boards; ~180 did); 1875: compulsory everywhere — water, sewers, health officers required. (Also: 1875 came after germ theory; 1848 before.)
  3. Searchers of the dead kept weekly bills of mortality; infected households were shut up (red cross, 40 days); (also: burial rules, cat/dog culls, public prayer days).
  4. The link between smoking and lung cancer (doctors’ study, 1950/54) — beginning evidence-based lifestyle policy.
  5. The 1866 cholera epidemic killed middle-class water-company customers — pressure became irresistible; Pasteur/Koch’s germ theory gave the scientific case for sanitation. (Also: 1867 Reform Act — urban voters now mattered.)
  6. Plan: attitudes (laissez-faire) blocked action 1842–66; epidemics forced exceptions; 1867 democracy + germ theory removed the excuses. Judgement: attitude change was necessary but came FROM epidemics + evidence — argue the circular relationship and commit.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Medieval: by-laws + quarantine only; church care
  • 1842 Chadwick · 1848 permissive · 1854 Snow · 1866 last cholera · 1875 compulsory
  • Laissez-faire = the barrier; epidemics + reports + voters = the push
  • 1948 NHS; 1956 Clean Air Act (smog); 2007 smoking ban; 2018 sugar tax
  • Continuity: inequality in health outcomes from Chadwick (1842) to COVID (2020)

🗺️ In the Exam

Public health is the THEME — it can be asked about ANY period ("how far did the government "improve public health 1800–1900?" is a classic). The 5-step government story (1842→1848→1854→1866→1875) is your skeleton; flesh it with ONE detail per step. For long-period comparisons, judge by PROVIDER (who acts: church → town → state) and MECHANISM (by-law → optional → compulsory → universal service).

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: "Chadwick was a doctor" Correct: a poor-law reformer (bureaucrat) — his argument was ECONOMIC (sick workers cost money)
  • Wrong: "the 1875 Act built hospitals" Correct: it mandated water, sewers, inspection — hospital care came with the NHS (1948)
  • Wrong: "medieval towns had no public health at all" Correct: quarantine, by-laws and street-cleaning existed — they were local, temporary and theory-driven (miasma)

🧭 Board Focus — OCR

Spec J410 — two routes: History A (Explaining the Modern World) and History B (Schools History Project). Your route decides the topics; check which route your centre takes. OCR's depth-study questions reward precise narrative chains — cause, event, consequence in order.

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Memorise the five-step government story as a chain — each step CAUSES the next
  • Provider-mechanism grid (church → town → state; by-law → optional → compulsory) answers any long-period comparison
  • End factor essays with the "who delivers?" question — knowledge vs delivery is the examiners’ favourite tension
