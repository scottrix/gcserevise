H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

Vesalius to Fleming: the individuals, the institutions that enabled them, and the individuals-versus-institutions debate — with pairing techniques for factor essays.

📋 Key Concepts: Individuals vs Institutions

Examiners love the individual-versus-others debate. Learn each figure WITH their enabling context — nobody innovates in a vacuum:

IndividualInnovationWhat made it possible
Vesalius (1543)Human anatomy correctedPrinting + humanist attitudes + dissection licences
Harvey (1628)Circulation provedExperiment method; Royal Society channels later
Sydenham (1670s)Bedside classificationGalen’s authority weakened
Jenner (1796)VaccinationFolk cowpox belief + Royal Society publication
Pasteur (1861)Germ theoryFrench brewing problem + better microscopes
Koch (1870s–80s)Microbe identificationPetri dish + photographic plate + German labs
Ehrlich (1909)Salvarsan "magic bullet"Koch’s dye-staining research
Fleming/Florey/Chain (1928/39–45)PenicillinWWII funding + US industry
Watson/Crick/Franklin (1953)DNA structureX-ray crystallography + team competition
Doll & Hill (1950/54)Smoking–cancer linkLarge-scale statistical method

The counterweight: institutions — universities, the Royal Society, German laboratories, the NHS, pharmaceutical companies — turn individual insight into medicine. Teamwork replaced lone genius after 1900 (penicillin, DNA, COVID vaccines).

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

"Individuals achieved more than institutions in medicine since 1500." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

YES side: Vesalius, Jenner, Pasteur, Fleming — named leaps, each against inertia. NO side: printing, the Royal Society, German labs, the NHS, the HGP — institutions created the CONDITIONS and the DELIVERY. Judgement: individuals for the leap, institutions for the landing — after 1900 the balance tips to teams. Use the tipping point as your verdict line.

Describe two features of Koch’s work (4).

« Koch identified the specific bacteria causing TB (1882) and cholera (1883) using new staining and culturing techniques — the Petri dish made pure cultures possible. His methods turned germ theory into a microbe-hunting programme other scientists could copy. »

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Give two reasons Harvey’s circulation theory was initially rejected.

Q2: Describe two features of Pasteur’s germ theory work.

Q3: Name the first "magic bullet" and the disease it treated.

Q4: Explain why Fleming’s discovery needed Florey and Chain (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: "The most important medical individual of the period c1250–present was Pasteur." How far do you agree? (16 + 4 marks)

✅ Answers

  1. Galen’s authority (teachers ridiculed Harvey); the idea seemed absurd (blood returning via invisible capillaries — not visible until microscopes improved); (also: no practical benefit at first).
  2. Disproved spontaneous generation (swan-neck flask, 1861); linked SPECIFIC microbes to SPECIFIC diseases, then delivered vaccines (chicken cholera, anthrax, rabies 1885).
  3. Salvarsan 606 (1909, Ehrlich) — treated syphilis.
  4. Fleming had no chemical-engineering route to mass production and published without developing it; Florey and Chain (Oxford, then US wartime funding) converted it into a stable, mass-producible drug — science needed industry + war.
  5. Plan: YES — germ theory underpins vaccination, antiseptic logic, Koch, Ehrlich, modern microbiology. NO — Jenner preceded him (vaccination without germ theory); NHS/delivery matters more to lives than any single scientist. Verdict: weigh "foundational knowledge" against "lives saved now" and commit.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Pair every individual with their context factor — the pairing IS the analytical point
  • After 1900: teams + institutions (Oxford penicillin team, HGP, mRNA collaborations)
  • Salvarsan 1909 → prontosil 1932 (Domagk) → penicillin 1928/39–45 = the drug pipeline
  • Nightingale and Garrett Anderson (1865, first woman on the UK medical register) for the women-in-medicine angle

🗺️ In the Exam

"Describe two features of X" questions on individuals need: (1) the innovation, (2) a specific detail (date, book, method). "How far" questions on individuals vs factors: use TWO individuals for, TWO factors against, and a judgement that names the tipping point (usually 1850–1900, when science + state began outpacing lone pioneers).

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: "Jenner discovered vaccination using germ theory" Correct: 1796 — a full 65 years BEFORE germ theory (he built on folk cowpox belief and experiment)
  • Wrong: "Pasteur and Koch worked together" Correct: bitter Franco-German RIVALS — the rivalry sped both up

🧭 Board Focus — OCR

Spec J410 — two routes: History A (Explaining the Modern World) and History B (Schools History Project). Your route decides the topics; check which route your centre takes. OCR's depth-study questions reward precise narrative chains — cause, event, consequence in order.

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Learn 5 individuals DEEPLY (Vesalius, Jenner, Pasteur, Koch, Fleming) rather than 10 thinly
  • Always attach the enabling factor: "X made the leap BECAUSE Y existed"
  • Women-in-medicine facts (Nightingale 1854+, Garrett Anderson 1865, women GPs after the 1877 Act loophole, WWII women doctors) are increasingly examined — keep two examples ready
