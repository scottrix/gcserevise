H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

The rebellions of 1068–70, the Harrying of the North, motte-and-bailey castles, land confiscation and the Revolt of the Earls — how the Normans took and kept England.

📋 Key Concepts: How the Normans Took and Kept England

1066–69 rebellions: Exeter (1068), Harold’s sons’ raid (1068), the northern rising (1069) — Edgar Aetheling + Northumbrians + Danish fleet — answered by the Harrying of the North (1069–70). The Revolt of the Earls (1075) was the last major internal rising (Waltheof executed 1076).

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

"Land confiscation was more important than castles in establishing Norman control." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

Castles: immediate, military, visible. Land: permanent, economic, generational — the new elite had everything to lose. Judgement: castles controlled the FIRST decade; landholding secured the NEXT century — "castles the bluntest instrument, landholding the deepest".

Describe two features of the Harrying of the North (4).

« Crops, herds and tools were destroyed between York and Durham — Domesday records villages as "waste" twenty years later. Refugees died of famine that winter; Orderic Vitalis claims 100,000+ deaths and calls William a "great tormentor". »

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Describe two features of the revolts of 1068–69.

Q2: Give two reasons motte-and-bailey castles suited the conquest.

Q3: Describe two features of the Revolt of the Earls (1075).

Q4: Explain why Norman control survived after 1070 (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: "The harrying was William’s greatest mistake." How far do you agree? (16 + 4 marks)

✅ Answers

  1. Exeter 1068 (Gytha — 18-day siege); 1069 northern rising (Edgar + Danes — Durham garrison murdered).
  2. Speed (earth-timber in days); garrison economy (small force overawes a district).
  3. Three earls (Ralph de Gael, Roger de Breteuil, Waltheof) plotted during William’s absence; crushed by loyal bishops; Waltheof executed 1076 — last English earl.
  4. Castle network garrisoned with loyal knights; elite replacement — no leaders left; (Danish fleet bought off 1070).
  5. Plan — YES: northern resistance permanently broken. NO: economy crippled (less tax); reputation damaged; castles/landholding did the holding. Verdict: harrying punished, castles governed.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Exeter 1068 · northern rising 1069 · Harrying 1069–70 · Earls 1075 · Waltheof executed 1076
  • Land 5% English by 1086 · 3,000+ motte-and-baileys · 500+ stone by 1100
  • Murdrum 46 marks · forest law · Domesday "waste" entries

🗺️ In the Exam

Match each control method to its DATE: 1066 shock → 1067 consolidation → 1068–69 risings → 1069–70 harrying → 1070s institutional grip. Precise sequencing earns marks over lists.

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: "stone castles from 1066" Correct: earth-timber first; stone keeps from the 1070s–90s
  • Wrong: "Waltheof was Norman" Correct: last ENGLISH earl, executed 1076

🧭 Board Focus — OCR

Spec J410 — two routes: History A (Explaining the Modern World) and History B (Schools History Project). Your route decides the topics; check which route your centre takes. OCR's depth-study questions reward precise narrative chains — cause, event, consequence in order.

Always check: specification details change — confirm paper structure and set texts against the current specification for your exam year.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Order control factors by DURATION: harrying (event) → castles (years) → landholding (generations)
  • Quote Domesday "waste" as documentary evidence of the harrying
