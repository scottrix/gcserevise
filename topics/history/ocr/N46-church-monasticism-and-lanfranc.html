H1: Kaiser Wilhelm and Germany

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

Lanfranc's councils, Norman bishops, Romanesque cathedrals beside castles, monastic learning and William's control of appointments — reform with political purpose.

📋 Key Concepts: The Norman Church

Personnel: by 1087 only Wulfstan of Worcester among English bishops; Stigand deposed 1070; Lanfranc (Archbishop 1070–89) — councils (1070, 1075), sees moved to towns, celibacy enforced, cathedral schools. Buildings: Romanesque cathedrals beside castles (Durham 1093). Monasticism: Cluniac Lewes (1077), monks as bishops. Rome: William controlled appointments — no homage to the pope demanded. Anselm vs Rufus — the First Quarrel (exile 1097).

🧮 Worked Examples — Exam Style

"The Norman Church was reformed more for political than religious reasons." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

POLITICAL: bishops replaced (control); cathedral-castle pairing; William’s appointments; reform removed Stigand (old-regime symbol). RELIGIOUS: genuine Gregorian ideals (celibacy, simony), monastic learning, Lanfranc’s scholarship. Verdict: religious methods, political outcomes.

Describe two features of Lanfranc’s reforms (4).

« Councils of 1070 and 1075 enforced celibacy and moved bishops’ sees to towns. He replaced irregular appointments and built cathedral schools training Norman administrators. »

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Who was the only English bishop to survive, and where?

Q2: Describe two features of Norman Romanesque architecture.

Q3: What did the 1075 Council rule about sees?

Q4: Explain why William replaced English bishops (two reasons, 4 marks).

Q5: "Lanfranc did more to secure the conquest than any baron." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

✅ Answers

  1. Wulfstan of Worcester — the exception, and a model of holiness the Normans used to validate their rule.
  2. Massive round-arched (Romanesque) naves/towers in stone; cathedrals sited in castle towns (Durham beside the castle).
  3. Sees moved from villages to towns — administration where power lay.
  4. Loyalty (English prelates backed risings in 1069); legitimacy (reforming the "corrupt" Church justified the conquest to Rome).
  5. Plan: the Church taxed, taught, recorded and preached obedience — every baronial skill with better paperwork.

📌 Key Facts to Memorise

  • Stigand deposed 1070 · Lanfranc 1070–89 · Anselm 1093 · Wulfstan survives
  • Councils 1070/1075 · sees to towns · celibacy · Durham begun 1093
  • William’s control of appointments — no homage to the pope

🗺️ In the Exam

Church questions link to CONTROL (legitimacy), ADMINISTRATION (literacy) and ARCHITECTURE (power in stone) — Durham’s castle-cathedral pairing is the ready example.

⚡ Targeted Drills

  • Lewes 1077 = first Cluniac priory; Battle Abbey = penance on the battlefield.
  • Lanfranc’s Constitutions c1077 = monastic rulebook for Christ Church.
  • Tithe compulsory; parish boundaries fixed (still traceable).
  • William 1080: no fealty to the pope — payment and permission only.
  • Anselm vs Rufus = the First Quarrel; exile 1097–1100.

📖 Detailed Notes: Monasticism and the Monastic Constitution

  • New houses: Lewes (Cluniac, 1077 — first priory of a French mother house), Battle Abbey (on Hastings field itself — William’s penance-vow, 1070s), Durham’s monastic cathedral. Monks became bishops (Walkelin at Winchester, Gundulf at Rochester) — the "monastic bishop" was a Norman signature.
  • Lanfranc’s Constitutions (c1077): a handbook for Christ Church, Canterbury — claustral discipline, obediencies, schooling for boys in the cloister. It spread across English monasteries and is our best window into monastic daily life (prayers, work, reading in strict rotation).
  • Parish revolution: tithe enforced as a compulsory tenth of produce; parish boundaries fixed (many still trace them); baptism/marriage/burial regulated — the Church penetrated village life more deeply after 1066 than before.
  • William vs the Pope: Gregory VII demanded fealty (1080); William refused but promised payment of Peter’s Pence and no papal letters in England without royal permission — "I consented to be his liege man for my salvation; fealty for my kingdom I never promised". Royal control of the Church, unusually strong in Europe.
  • Anselm: a scholar-saint (Proslogion, Cur Deus Homo) forced on Canterbury in 1093; his quarrel with Rufus over investiture and the schismatic popes (Urban vs Clement) sent him into exile — the template for Becket’s later conflict.

⚠️ Common Errors

  • Wrong: "Lanfranc was a puppet" Correct: a genuine Gregorian reformer serving both pope and king
  • Wrong: "Anselm served Rufus loyally" Correct: quarrelled and exiled himself (1097–1100)

✍️ Extended Answer Plan — "The Norman Church served the conqueror more than God." How far do you agree? (16 + 4)

CONQUEROR: English bishops replaced; cathedral-castle siting; Stigand’s deposition as conquest-legitimacy; William’s appointments control.

GOD: genuine Gregorian reform (celibacy, simony); monastic learning and the Constitutions; tithe-funded charity; Anselm’s willingness to exile himself for principle.

Judgement: separate the INSTRUMENT (the Church as government partner) from the FAITH (real reform ideals). Conclude: Lanfranc served two masters and believed he was serving one — the ambiguity is the answer.

🔬 Case Study Focus — Durham: Castle, Cathedral and the North

Durham’s peninsula — river on three sides — holds castle (1072) and cathedral (1093) side by side: the message needs no words. The cathedral pioneered rib-vaulting and massive geometric pillars (Europe’s first Gothic experiment) — Norman engineering at its peak. Its bishops were prince-bishops (palatinate powers: their own sheriffs, courts and coinage) ruling a troubled frontier. Exam use: Durham answers military, spiritual AND administrative questions — one site, three functions, all datable.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Durham = castle + cathedral in one town — military and spiritual dominance together
  • Stigand’s deposition = the symbolic start of reform
