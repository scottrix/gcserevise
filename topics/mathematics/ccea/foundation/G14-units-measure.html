G14: Units of Measure

Foundation Higher AQAEdexcelOCREduqasCCEA

Use standard units of measure and related concepts; length, area, volume, capacity, mass, time, money

📋 Key Concepts

Units of measure are standard quantities used to measure physical quantities like length, mass, time, etc.

📝 Length

Metric units: km (kilometre), m (metre), cm (centimetre), mm (millimetre)
1 km = 1000 m
1 m = 100 cm
1 cm = 10 mm
1 km = 100,000 cm
Example 1

Convert 3.5 km to metres.

Solution: 3.5 × 1000 = 3500 m

Example 2

Convert 4500 mm to metres.

Solution: 4500 ÷ 1000 = 4.5 m

📝 Area

Metric units: km², m², cm², mm², hectares (ha)
1 km² = 1,000,000 m²
1 m² = 10,000 cm²
1 cm² = 100 mm²
1 hectare = 10,000 m²
Important: Area units are squared, so conversion factors are also squared!
Example 3

Convert 5 m² to cm².

Solution: 5 × 10,000 = 50,000 cm²

Example 4

Convert 30,000 cm² to m².

Solution: 30,000 ÷ 10,000 = 3 m²

📝 Volume

Metric units: km³, m³, cm³, mm³, litres (L), millilitres (mL)
1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³
1 cm³ = 1000 mm³
1 litre = 1000 mL
1 cm³ = 1 mL
Example 5

Convert 2.5 m³ to cm³.

Solution: 2.5 × 1,000,000 = 2,500,000 cm³

Example 6

Convert 3500 mL to litres.

Solution: 3500 ÷ 1000 = 3.5 L

📝 Mass

Metric units: tonne (t), kilogram (kg), gram (g), milligram (mg)
1 tonne = 1000 kg
1 kg = 1000 g
1 g = 1000 mg
Example 7

Convert 0.5 tonnes to grams.

Solution:

0.5 tonnes = 500 kg

500 kg = 500,000 g

📝 Time

Time units: hours (h), minutes (min), seconds (s)
1 hour = 60 minutes
1 minute = 60 seconds
1 hour = 3600 seconds
Example 8

Convert 2.5 hours to minutes.

Solution: 2.5 × 60 = 150 minutes

Example 9

Convert 90 minutes to hours.

Solution: 90 ÷ 60 = 1.5 hours

📝 Imperial-Metric Conversions

Approximate conversions:
ImperialMetric
1 inch≈ 2.5 cm
1 foot≈ 30 cm
1 mile≈ 1.6 km
1 pound (lb)≈ 450 g
1 gallon≈ 4.5 litres

❓ Practice Questions

Q1: Convert 7500 m to km.

Q2: Convert 0.8 m² to cm².

Q3: Convert 3 litres to mL.

Q4: Convert 2.4 kg to grams.

Q5: Convert 135 minutes to hours.

✅ Answers

  1. 7.5 km
  2. 8000 cm²
  3. 3000 mL
  4. 2400 g
  5. 2.25 hours (or 2 hours 15 minutes)

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Learn the conversion factors by heart
  • Remember: area units are squared, volume units are cubed
  • 1 cm³ = 1 mL is very useful for volume problems
  • For time, use fractions: 45 min = 3/4 hour = 0.75 hours
  • Check your answer is sensible - should it be bigger or smaller?
  • Practise both metric-metric and imperial-metric conversions

🧠 Problem-Solving Strategies

Problem-Solving

Learn key conversions: 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 litre = 1000 ml, 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 tonne = 1000 kg. For area: 1 m squared = 10,000 cm squared. For volume: 1 m cubed = 1,000,000 cm cubed. Always convert to the same unit before calculating. Use dimensional analysis to check answers.
Multi-Step Problem

A garden measures 12 m by 8 m. Fertiliser is applied at 25 g per square metre and costs £3.50 per kg. Calculate the total cost of fertiliser for the garden.

Solution: Area = 12 x 8 = 96 m squared. Fertiliser needed = 96 x 25 = 2400 g = 2.4 kg. Cost = 2.4 x 3.50 = £8.40.

⚠️ Common Errors

Watch Out!

1. Wrong: Converting 2 m squared to cm squared by multiplying by 100 Correct: 2 m squared = 2 x 10,000 = 20,000 cm squared. For area, multiply by 100 squared = 10,000, not 100.

2. Wrong: Converting 3 m cubed to cm cubed by multiplying by 100 Correct: 3 m cubed = 3 x 1,000,000 = 3,000,000 cm cubed. For volume, multiply by 100 cubed = 1,000,000.

3. Wrong: Mixing imperial and metric units without conversion (e.g. adding feet and metres) Correct: Always convert all measurements to the same unit system before adding or comparing. 1 foot = 30.48 cm, 1 inch = 2.54 cm, 1 mile = 1.609 km.

✍️ 6-Mark Exam Question

Extended Answer

6 marks: A swimming pool is 25 m long, 10 m wide and 2 m deep at the deep end, sloping to 1 m at the shallow end. (a) Calculate the volume of water in the pool in m cubed. (b) Convert this to litres. (c) Water costs 0.2p per litre. Find the cost to fill the pool. The pool loses 2 cm of depth per week through evaporation. How many litres evaporate per week?

(a) Average depth = (2 + 1)/2 = 1.5 m. Volume = 25 x 10 x 1.5 = 375 m cubed.

(b) 375 m cubed = 375 x 1000 = 375,000 litres.

(c) Cost = 375,000 x 0.2p = 75,000p = £750.

Evaporation: 2 cm depth = 0.02 m. Volume per week = 25 x 10 x 0.02 = 5 m cubed = 5000 litres.

Mark scheme: M1 average depth, A1 volume 375, M1 conversion, A1 375000 litres, M1 cost, A1 £750, M1 evaporation volume, A1 5000 litres

📊 AO3: Reason & Interpret

Reasoning and Interpretation

A recipe requires 2 lb of flour and 1 pint of milk. A student has 1 kg of flour and 1 litre of milk.

(a) Does the student have enough flour? (1 lb = 454 g)

(b) Does the student have enough milk? (1 pint = 568 ml)

(c) The student says "I can just use 2 kg of flour instead of 2 lb since they're roughly the same." How far off would this be? Is this an acceptable approximation?

Answers: (a) 2 lb = 908 g = 0.908 kg. Yes, 1 kg is enough (92 g spare). (b) 1 pint = 568 ml. Yes, 1 litre (1000 ml) is enough (432 ml spare). (c) 2 kg instead of 2 lb = 2000 g instead of 908 g. That is 2.2 times too much flour. This is NOT an acceptable approximation — it would completely ruin the recipe. The student is confusing kg with lb.
